Saltwater Fishing on Long Island

What to target, when to go, and where to cast, from the Sound to the South Shore inlets.

Start exploring

The Main Catches

Five species make up most of what Long Islanders catch. Seasons below are the typical best windows. Open harvest seasons are set by the DEC and can be narrower, so check the current rules before you keep anything.

Best: May–June & Oct–Nov

Striped Bass

"Stripers"

The prize fish of Long Island. Big spring and fall migrations bring them close to beaches, jetties and inlets. Dawn, dusk and night tides are usually most productive.

Best: June–October

Bluefish

"Blues" / "Snappers" when small

Aggressive and hard-fighting, often chasing bait on the surface. Use a wire or heavy leader because their teeth cut through light line.

Best: May–September

Fluke

Summer flounder

Flatfish that lie on sandy bottoms and ambush bait. Drift over sand with a bucktail and squid strip or live killies. Popular in the bays and off the South Shore.

Best: June–October

Porgy

Scup

Plentiful, easy to catch, and good to eat. A great beginner fish. Small hooks baited with clam or squid on rocky or hard bottom, especially in Long Island Sound.

Best: October–December

Blackfish

Tautog

Structure-loving fish found around rocks, wrecks and jetties. Green or Asian crabs are the go-to bait. Expect to lose rigs, and set the hook fast.

Good Spots to Try

A starting list of well-known, publicly accessible places. Some state parks require a parking fee or a night-fishing permit, so check before you go.

North Shore

Long Island Sound: calmer water, rocky bottom

  • Sunken Meadow State Park & Nissequogue RiverRiver mouth and flats; stripers and blues on moving tides.
  • Port Jefferson Harbor & Mount Sinai JettiesJetties hold blackfish and porgies; stripers at the inlet.
  • Wildwood State ParkRocky beach with bluffs; good surf casting for stripers and blues.
  • Oyster Bay & Centre IslandProtected water, porgies in summer, and easy access for small boats.
  • Orient PointStrong currents at the tip of the North Fork, well known for stripers and blackfish.

South Shore

Atlantic beaches, bays and inlets

  • Jones Beach & Jones InletSurf and jetty fishing; fluke in the inlet, stripers in fall.
  • Captree State ParkPiers and a big party-boat fleet at Fire Island Inlet. Great for beginners.
  • Robert Moses State ParkOcean surf casting for stripers and blues during the fall run.
  • Shinnecock InletRock jetties with strong current; stripers, blues and blackfish.
  • Montauk PointThe famous rocky tip of the South Fork, with legendary fall striper and bluefish blitzes.

Beginner Tips

Keep it simple your first few trips.

  1. Start on a party boat. Boats out of Captree, Freeport or Montauk supply rods and bait, and the mates will show you how to use them.
  2. Watch the tides. Moving water, especially the first and last hours of a tide, is usually when fish feed. Slack tide is often slow.
  3. Use a versatile setup. A 7-foot medium spinning rod with 15–20 lb line handles porgies, fluke and school stripers.
  4. Bring a ruler and pliers. Measure every fish you plan to keep, and use pliers to unhook quickly and release fish in good shape.
  5. Ask at the local tackle shop. They know what's biting right now and what bait works this week. Buy a little bait while you're there.
  6. Stay safe on the rocks. Jetties are slick. Wear shoes with good grip, never turn your back on the surf, and check the weather.

License & Regulations

Before you fish, register. Anglers 16 and older need to enroll in New York's free Recreational Marine Fishing Registry to fish in the state's marine and coastal waters. Party-boat customers are usually covered by the boat's registration.

  • Size limits, bag limits and open seasons apply to every species on this page. They change often, sometimes mid-year.
  • Striped bass have a "slot" limit: only fish within a set length range may be kept.
  • Blackfish and fluke have limited open seasons; outside them, catch-and-release only.
  • Measure correctly and release undersized fish quickly and gently.
